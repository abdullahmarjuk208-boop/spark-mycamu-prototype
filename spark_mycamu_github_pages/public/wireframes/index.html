Spark (MyCamu) Wireframes

Fourteen annotated low-fidelity screens showing the redesigned student journey from access and academic orientation to assignment submission, recovery and durable confirmation.

Open the interactive prototype →
Design intent. The wireframes prioritise urgent academic work, reduce navigation ambiguity, prevent submission errors and keep evidence of completion visible. They were developed for the student persona Nadia Rahman and informed the responsive prototype.

1. Institution selection

Remembers the last institution and provides a clear change route.

Recognition replaces repeated institution search.

2. Student sign-in

Recommended SSO is prominent while help remains available.

Supports password managers, keyboard access and clear recovery.

3. Academic dashboard

Courses, CGPA, attendance and due work appear immediately.

The first view answers “What matters today?”

4. Courses

Familiar course codes and names support rapid recognition.

Course cards expose progress and next activity.

5. Course workspace

Materials, tasks and people remain grouped by course.

Current module and related task are visible together.

6. Prioritised tasks

Assignments are ordered by urgency and completion state.

Deadline, course and status are never separated.

7. Assignment details

Requirements, attempts, policy and deadline precede submission.

Students can judge readiness before committing.

8. Upload - empty state

Accepted formats and maximum size are visible before selection.

Instructions prevent avoidable upload errors.

9. Upload progress

Filename, size, percentage and connection protection are explicit.

Visibility of system status reduces uncertainty.

10. Upload error

An unsupported format explains the cause and direct recovery.

Progress is preserved; replace and valid-demo actions remain available.

11. Validation success

Format, size and upload completeness are individually confirmed.

A file is “ready to review,” not falsely labelled “submitted.”

12. Review submission

Preview, replace and remove remain available before confirmation.

Reversible review protects against wrong-file submission.

13. Final confirmation

The final consequence is restated before the committed action.

Assignment, filename and resulting status remain visible.

14. Receipt and history

A timestamp, reference and version timeline provide durable proof.

Completion evidence survives beyond a temporary notification.
